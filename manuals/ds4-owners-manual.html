DS-4 Owner's Manual

Personal datadeck for reading licensed data shards at home, at work and on the go.

DS-4 · KFW2 · ShardView 4.2

1 Welcome

Thank you for choosing the Kestrel DS-4. Your deck reads data shards: small sealed memories that carry text documents and voice recordings. Most shards you will receive are encrypted. Only the shard's owner, holding its 6-digit key, can open them.

Kestrel. We never lose sight.

2 Controls

1234567890ABCDEF* ESC# RETURN
  • 0 to 9 and A to F enter numbers and menu choices.
  • # (RETURN) confirms, turns pages forward and plays the next part of a recording.
  • * (ESC) goes back, deletes a typed character, or ejects from the main screen.
  • B turns a page back.
  • If your deck is connected to a keyboard, Enter works as # and Backspace or Esc as *.

3 Reading a shard with ShardView

  1. Insert the shard in the SHARD slot. The deck reads it in a moment.
  2. ShardView shows what kind of shard it is and its status.
  3. Press 1 to read a text document or play a voice recording. Press 2 to eject.

Every DS-4 screen lists its keys at the bottom, as a key, a dash and what it does: 1-read means press 1 to read. The command line under the screen shows where you are and echoes what you type.

4 The AUX port

The AUX slot accepts licensed Kestrel modules. When a module is plugged in, the deck hands its screen and keypad to that module. Unplug it to return to ShardView.

Caution. Unlicensed cartridges sold in night markets may harm your deck, your data and your standing with your block warden. Kestrel accepts no responsibility for modified shards. Using such software to open a shard without its owner's key may be illegal where you live.

5 Expansion bays

The DS-4 has 2 expansion bays on its underside. Each bay holds one Kestrel-certified hardware module, and a module works with any software running on the deck. Remove a module to make room for another.

Ask your Kestrel retailer which modules suit the way you use your deck.

6 Temperature

The TEMP readout under the command line shows the deck's temperature in °C. The DS-4 warms up when it works hard and cools down by itself when idle.

  • At 70 °C the OVERHEAT light comes on. It blinks as the deck nears its limit.
  • At 85 °C the deck shuts itself off to protect its circuits. The keys stop working.
  • The blue bar beside the readout fills as the deck cools. At 45 °C it restarts on its own, with any module still in place.

Tip. Some third-party software runs the DS-4 much harder than it was designed for. If your deck keeps shutting down, give it time between tasks.
